# Contribution 1 — design document: within-match opponent inference for N-player imperfect-information games

*Chapter 15, September 2026. Gap wording from `deliverables/finalReview/lit_gaps.md` (C1,
verified 2026-09-24). Numbers from the result files named in brackets. This document replaces
the plan's C1 table (`planning/rawSteps/step_15…`, Phase 4 Day 1), whose gap statement ("no
unified detect → adapt → evaluate pipeline") no longer holds.*

## 1. Problem

An agent sits at a table of an imperfect-information game with two or more opponents. It sees
their actions and, only when a hand is shown down, their private cards. It must infer, *during
the match*, how each opponent plays — including when an opponent changes strategy part-way
through — quickly and reliably enough that the inference can drive a bounded deviation from an
equilibrium-based strategy (Contribution 2), and the result must be measurable with a protocol
that is the same across games (Contribution 3).

Research question (Chapter I, § 1.7, kept verbatim): **RQ1.** *How can an agent infer, during a
match, the strategies of several opponents in an imperfect-information game — including shifts
within the match — fast and reliably enough to act on the inference?*

Refinement proposed here: "reliably enough to act on" is made operational as **calibration of the
model's confidence**, because Contribution 2 scales the size of the deviation by that confidence.
An inference that is fast but over-confident is worse than a slow calibrated one.

## 2. Prior art (what exists)

- **Bayesian and frequency models in poker.** Hidden cards marginalised at showdown (Southey et
  al. 2005); payoff against a posterior equals payoff against its mean (Ganzfried & Sun 2018);
  consistency of a sequence-form MAP estimator for a static opponent (Ganzfried 2025, preprint).
- **Detect → adapt with a conservative fallback, against switching opponents.** GSCU keeps a
  posterior over an opponent embedding and switches between a greedy and a conservative policy
  with a bandit, with a regret guarantee; tested on Kuhn poker (Fu et al., ICML 2022). PACE adds a
  peer-identification reward (Ma et al., ICML 2024).
- **In-context and sequence models.** TAO, OMIS, OEOM (Jing et al. 2024a, 2024b, 2025); in poker,
  StratFormer (Caen et al., CG 2026, preprint) and AlphaExploitem (Murgoci et al. 2026, preprint)
  model and exploit while staying near equilibrium, heads-up only.
- **Opponent models inside equilibrium search**, model given: Milec et al. (AAMAS 2024, 2025).
- **N-player:** opponent modelling in three-player Kuhn beats equilibrium strategies, with no
  safety notion (Ganzfried, Wang & Chiswick, DAI 2024); style features in multi-player hold'em
  (Shi et al. 2025).
- **Representation from logs:** player2vec-style embeddings; behavioural cloning; clustering of
  playing styles (Chapter 13).

## 3. The gap (lit_gaps wording — may be narrowed, never widened)

Real-time opponent modelling with a conservative fallback exists and works in two-player games,
including poker [Fu 2022; Caen 2026; Murgoci 2026], and opponent models have entered
depth-limited search [Milec 2025]. All of it is two-player; N-player opponent modelling exists
without any safety criterion [Ganzfried 2024]. **Open:** online inference in N-player
imperfect-information games that handles strategy shifts *within* a match and is coupled to an
explicit safety criterion and a common evaluation protocol.

C1 therefore does **not** claim the detect–adapt loop. It claims its extension to N players, to
within-match shifts, and its coupling to C2's safety criterion and C3's protocol.

## 4. Method (what the thesis will build)

1. **Per-opponent Dirichlet model over information sets** with hidden cards handled by
   posterior-weighted soft counts (Chapter 7's continuous model; Chapter 14's DirBR3P extends it
   to two opponents). It is type-free, so it cannot be "confidently wrong about the menu", the
   failure measured in Chapter 7.
2. **A type prior learned from logs.** Chapter 13's four k-means styles and its player
   embedding give an informative prior (a small number of pseudo-counts per information set,
   mapped through the style of the nearest cluster), so the first 50–100 hands are not spent on a
   uniform prior. The prior's weight is a pre-registered hyper-parameter.
3. **Within-match change detection with a calibrated reaction.** Bayesian online change-point
   detection per opponent on several signals (not one aggression signal: Chapter 14 found that the
   Kuhn-tuned detector fires on stationary Leduc opponents), and a *partial* reset (discount old
   counts) instead of a full reset. The false-alarm rate on stationary opponents is a reported
   quantity, not a tuning afterthought.
4. **Confidence as an output.** The posterior predictive variance of the model's value for the
   deviation (or simply the effective sample size n/(n + n₀) used in the pilots) is passed to C2's
   deviation rule, and its calibration is measured (predicted vs realised gain per 100-hand
   window).
5. **Collusion signals as part of inference** (the link to C2's coalition-aware response):
   Chapter 13's per-player standardised soft-play and chip-dumping scores, computed online over
   the pairs at the table.

## 5. Experiments

Experiment 1.1 (`experiments.md`): within-match adaptation on two- and three-player Kuhn and
Leduc against stationary, switching and teaching opponents, under Chapter 14's protocol (gain,
capture, h50, r50, false-alarm rate, calibration, and C2's loss readouts). Experiment 1.2
(real-data arm): the same model run online over Chapter 13's IPN hand histories — time to a
confident type, stability, re-identification — and on Playtech data if access is granted.

## 9. Risks and mitigation

| Risk | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| The two-player literature moves into N players before 2028 (StratFormer and AlphaExploitem list it as future work) | medium | publish the N-player, within-match, safety-coupled evaluation early (Paper 4 in 2027); keep the claim to the coupling and the protocol, which are the parts nobody has |
| Hidden cards make N-player inference slow (two unseen hands) | medium | three-player Kuhn first (exact), soft counts, a log-learned prior; report hands-to-confidence rather than claim speed |
| Change detection misfires (as on Leduc in Chapter 14) | high | multi-signal detection, partial resets, false-alarm rate as a pre-registered readout |
| No labelled real data (IPN 2009 has no labels; Playtech access uncertain) | high | injected colluders and Pluribus as ground truth (Chapter 13); treat Playtech data as a bonus, not a dependency |
| LLM agents make "opponent modelling" a moving target | low for the thesis | LLMs stay out of scope except as extracted fixed strategies in the zoo (Chapter 12) |
