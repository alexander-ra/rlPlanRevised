# Contribution 3 — design document: a joint evaluation protocol for adaptive agents

*Chapter 15, September 2026. Gap wording from `lit_gaps.md` (C3) and the nine failure modes of
`lit_evaluation.md`. The plan's C3 table ("no framework combines exploitability + ranking +
confidence"; "no cross-game validation"; "O(n²) scaling") is withdrawn: all three statements were
disproved by the September 2026 check.*

## 1. Problem

How should an agent that *adapts* — leaves equilibrium on purpose, changes during a match, plays
against several opponents who may coordinate — be evaluated, so that what it gains, how fast, and
what it risks are measured together, with confidence, and the same way in every game?

Research question (Chapter I, § 1.7, kept): **RQ3.** *How should adaptive agents be evaluated so
that gain, speed of adaptation and worst-case (coalition-aware) robustness are measured together,
with confidence, and unchanged across different games?*

Refinement proposed here (from Pilot P1, `EXECUTION_NOTES.md`): the worst-case readout needs two
time scales. The **exposure of the policy in force** (Chapter 14) is the right per-hand quantity
for agents with a fixed deviation rule, but a gift-banking agent such as RWYWE is safe *over the
match* while spending banked gains after a switch; judged per hand after the switch it looks
unsafe. The protocol therefore adds **match-level safety** — the mean over the whole match of the
agent's expected payoff minus the safety reference (v* with two players; the maximin value or a
baseline with more) — which is exactly Ganzfried & Sandholm's definition of safety (Def. 4.1).

## 2. Prior art

Worst case: exact exploitability and accelerated best response (Johanson et al. 2011), LBR
(Lisý & Bowling 2017), learned best responses (Timbers et al. 2022); NashConv for N players
(Lanctot et al. 2019). Head-to-head with variance reduction: duplicate play, DIVAT, AIVAT (Burch,
Schmid, Moravčík, Morrill & Bowling 2018), anytime-valid AIVAT (Li et al. 2026, preprint), AIVAT
pathologies (Kim & Sandholm 2026, preprint). Populations: Elo, TrueSkill, Nash averaging and
"Re-evaluating evaluation" (Balduzzi et al. 2018), α-Rank (Omidshafiei et al. 2019), VasE (Lanctot
et al., arXiv 2023/2025), deviation ratings (Marris et al. 2025), EGTA (Wellman 2006; Tuyls et al.
2020), the transitive/cyclic decomposition (Balduzzi et al. 2019) and spinning tops (Czarnecki et
al. 2020). Generalisation: Melting Pot (Leibo et al. 2021). The nearest precedent for joint
evaluation: the repeated rock–paper–scissors benchmark (Lanctot et al., TMLR 2023), which scores
population return and within-population exploitability together, for one two-player game.

## 3. The gap (lit_gaps wording)

Evaluation is well developed along separate axes; one benchmark combines population return and
exploitability, for one two-player game [Lanctot 2023b]. **Open:** a protocol that reports gain,
adaptation speed and recovery, and worst-case or coalition-aware robustness, with confidence
bounds, applied unchanged across several imperfect-information games including N-player ones.

The claim is framed as *"existing evaluation breaks in these settings, and the protocol catches
what it misses"* (plan note P4*), never as "we assembled a toolkit".

## 4. Method — the protocol (Chapter 14, extended here)

For an agent under test, per game, against a zoo (equilibria, rule-based types, LLM-extracted
strategies, static specialists, adaptive agents) plus switching opponents, a white-box teaching
attack and — with three or more players — independent, fixed-colluding and adaptively colluding
pairs:

| Readout | Definition | Games |
|---|---|---|
| Gain, capture | expected payoff minus the blueprint's against the same opponent, and its share of the exact best response's gain | all |
| Speed h50, recovery r50 | first hand at which capture ≥ 0.5 and stays there over the next 100 hands; the same after a switch | all |
| Exposure | two players: v* minus the worst case of the policy in force; N players: the exact coalition value, or the baseline-relative worst case L(σ) | exact where enumerable; learned exploiter with stated budget elsewhere |
| Match-level safety S | mean over the match of (EV_t − safety reference); fraction of matches with S < 0 | all (new in Chapter 15) |
| Teaching loss | mean shortfall after a bait-then-best-respond switch (coalition version for N players) | all |
| Confidence | 95 % intervals over seeds with the estimator named (raw chips / AIVAT / policy-exact) and hands to a stated precision | all |
| Population context | Elo, Nash averaging, α-Rank, VasE, RRPS score, spinning top — reported, not used to rank | all |

## 9. Risks and mitigation

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Novelty objection: "a combination of known metrics" | lead with the failure-mode matrix — each existing metric misses a named, reproduced failure; RRPS cited as the nearest precedent and the difference stated (N-player, coalition-aware, within-match speed/recovery, match-level safety) |
| Exact worst cases only exist in toy games | report learned exploiters as lower bounds with their budget (failure mode 3); calibrate learned coalition exploiters against the exact value on three-player Kuhn |
| Per-window estimates need too many hands without AIVAT | AIVAT with the agent's own strategy known; anytime-valid stopping (Li et al. 2026) evaluated as an option |
| So Long Sucker stays uninformative (alliances cost a focal baseline only 0.8–1.9 points in Chapter 14; ~99.5 % of random games deadlock in Chapter 11) | drop SLS as a C3 testbed unless the engine is fixed; three-player Leduc is the N-player testbed of record |
| Horizon and population choices drive rankings | fix and report them; the protocol's primary readouts do not depend on the population's composition |
