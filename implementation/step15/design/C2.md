# Contribution 2 — design document: exploitation with a checked loss bound beyond two players

*Chapter 15, September 2026 — the thesis centrepiece. Gap wording from `lit_gaps.md` (C2, "open
at the core, narrowed around it"). This replaces the plan's C2 table, whose objective ("exploit
while guaranteeing at least equal share") is provably unattainable in the cases that matter, and
whose mechanism ("piKL keeps the policy near Nash") misdescribes piKL.*

## 1. Problem

An agent plays a small imperfect-information game with two or more opponents. It starts from an
equilibrium-based strategy (the blueprint) and has an online model of its opponents (C1). It
wants to exploit the opponents' mistakes. How far may it deviate from the blueprint, and what can
it promise about the cost of deviating — including when two opponents coordinate against it?

Research question (Chapter I, § 1.7): **RQ2.** *How can deviation from an equilibrium baseline be
bounded in N-player imperfect-information games so that exploitation gain is traded against a
stated, empirically verifiable loss bound, also against coordinating opponents?*

Refinement proposed here: RQ2 is answered with **two kinds of bound**, because with three players
no single reference plays the role of the two-player game value v*:

- an **absolute** bound against a coordinated pair — the team-maxmin value v_mm (the most a seat
  can guarantee against any correlated strategy of the other two) as the floor of Ganzfried and
  Sandholm's gift accounting; and
- a **baseline-relative** bound — the most the agent can fall behind the blueprint against *any*
  opponent pair, L(σ), capped per hand.

## 2. Prior art

- **Two-player safe exploitation** (all two-player zero-sum): ε-safe responses and RNR (Johanson,
  Zinkevich & Bowling 2007); gift-based safety over the repeated game, RWYWE, BEFFE, BEFEWP
  (Ganzfried & Sandholm, ACM TEAC 2015); prime-safe (Jeary & Turrini 2023, preprint); SES (Liu et
  al., NeurIPS 2022); adaptation safety, OX-Search (Ge, Xu, Ding, Meng, An, Li & Gao, ICML 2024);
  beyond the depth limit (Milec, Kovařík & Lisý, AAMAS 2025); per-deployment certificates (Li &
  Huang 2026, preprint); bounded test-time RL (Kubíček, Lisý & Sandholm 2026, preprint);
  baseline-relative regret (Müller et al., ICML 2025).
- **N-player safety notions**: equal share C/n, achievable only against identical, slowly adapting
  opponents and provably not securable otherwise (Ge, Wang, Li & Jin, ICML 2025, Props. 4.1–4.2);
  team-maxmin against a coordinated coalition (Celli & Gatti 2018; Zhang, An & Černý 2021; Zhang,
  Farina & Sandholm 2023). Ganzfried & Sandholm (2015, § 2.3) remark that their method "applies
  straightforwardly" to multiplayer games with the maximin value — a remark, not evaluated.
- **N-player exploitation without a safety notion**: opponent modelling in three-player Kuhn
  (Ganzfried, Wang & Chiswick 2024); style-conditioned multi-player hold'em (Shi et al. 2025).
- **KL anchoring**: piKL and DiL-piKL anchor to an imitation-learned human policy, for
  compatibility, with no loss bound (Jacob et al. 2022; Bakhtin et al. 2023). Anchoring to a
  blueprint is the thesis's own proposal; two-player relatives are RNR's mixture, magnetic mirror
  descent and StratFormer's schedule.
- **Coalitions and collusion**: synchronous vs asynchronous coalitions (Babyak et al. 2024,
  preprint); utility transfer inside the three-player Kuhn equilibrium family (Szafron, Gibson &
  Sturtevant 2013); collusion detection scored offline (Mazrooei et al. 2013; Bonjour et al. 2022;
  Greige et al. 2022; Xu et al. 2025).

## 3. The gap (lit_gaps wording)

Safe exploitation has a mature two-player zero-sum theory. With three or more players the existing
notions are very conservative (team-maxmin) or provably unattainable against heterogeneous
opponents (equal share), KL anchoring is behavioural, and multiplayer exploitation has no loss
bound. **Open (no counter-example found):** exploiting sub-optimal opponents in N-player
imperfect-information games while bounding the loss relative to a baseline, and testing that bound
against colluding opponents. C2 targets this empirically, in small games; it claims no general
N-player safety theorem.

## 4. The non-claim (stated in every paper and in the dissertation)

- **No general N-player safety theorem** is claimed.
- The contribution is **empirical and in small games**. Every bound is either exact by
  construction (the capped mixture; the maximin-floor accounting, whose argument is Ganzfried and
  Sandholm's with the joint opponent in place of the single one) or **checked exactly** by
  enumeration where the game allows it; in larger games it is **estimated** with a learned
  coalition exploiter whose budget is stated.
- **Equal share is a reference line, not a guarantee.** Against a colluding pair — the
  heterogeneous case of Ge et al.'s Prop. 4.1 — no strategy can promise it.
- The maximin-floor idea is **Ganzfried and Sandholm's** (§ 2.3). What the thesis can add is the
  coordinated-pair floor, the exact checks, and the tests against fixed and adaptive colluders.

## 5. Method

Three deviation rules share C1's opponent model and the equilibrium blueprint:

1. **MM-RWYWE (absolute bound).** SAFE(k) = {σ : coalition value(σ) ≥ v_mm − k};
   π_t = argmax over SAFE(k_t) of the expected payoff against the model; k_1 = 0 and
   k_{t+1} = k_t + min over joint pure strategies of the other two consistent with hand t of
   u(π_t, ·) − v_mm. Guarantee: Σ_t E u(π_t, y_t) ≥ T·v_mm against any opponents, colluding and
   adaptive included. Implementation: sequence-form LP with constraint generation; the oracle
   enumerates the unique pure plans of the smaller opponent (6,561 or 10,000 in three-player Kuhn)
   and best-responds with the other. Two accounting variants: opponents' cards revealed (G&S's
   experimental assumption) and showdown only.
2. **BD(ε) (baseline-relative cap).** σ_t = (1 − λ_t)·blueprint + λ_t·BR(model), mixed in sequence
   form, with λ_t = conf_t · min(1, ε / L(BR_t)) and conf_t = n/(n + n₀). Then L(σ_t) = λ_t·L(BR_t)
   ≤ ε exactly: in every hand the agent is at most ε behind what the blueprint would earn against
   the same opponent strategies — whatever they are.
3. **KL(β) (behavioural anchor, measured).** π(a|I) ∝ blueprint(a|I)·exp(β_t·Q(I, a)), with Q the
   action value against the model in chips and β_t = β·conf_t. No a-priori bound; L(σ_t) is
   measured.

Coalition-aware extension (Experiment 2.2): while an online collusion detector (C1) flags a pair,
shrink ε, or fall back to the maximin strategy.

## 10. Risks and mitigation

| Risk | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Exact checks do not scale beyond three-player Kuhn | certain | three-player Leduc with a learned coalition exploiter (stated budget), calibrated against the exact values on three-player Kuhn |
| The pessimistic gift accounting banks too little when cards stay hidden (Pilot P1: see § 6) | high | report both accounting variants; within-hand gift detection (G&S § 8.3); partial observation models |
| The baseline itself is unsafe against colluders (the blueprint loses 0.119 chips/hand to a fixed pair, Chapter 14) | certain | report absolute and relative readouts; the maximin strategy as a second baseline |
| The maximin floor turns out very conservative in larger games | medium | three-player Kuhn suggests otherwise (§ 6), but the claim is checked in Leduc before it is made |
| Someone publishes an N-player safe exploiter first | medium | the September 2026 check found none; re-run the forward search from OX-Search and equal share every six months |
| KL anchoring is misread as piKL | low | cite piKL for what it is; present the blueprint anchor as the thesis's proposal |
